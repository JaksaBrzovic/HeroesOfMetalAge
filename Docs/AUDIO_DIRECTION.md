# Sound-first direction

Original dark heavy-metal atmosphere inspired by the user's references to Manowar and Bathory. Do not use their recordings or reproduce melodies in anything shared or published. Exception for private testing only: the current map music is a loop cut from Manowar's "Spirit Horse of the Cherokee" and must be replaced before any build leaves the developer's machine (see `AUDIO_CREDITS.md`). The ambient and gallop layers, the friendly/gold/equip/click/level/heavy-hit cues and the unit voices are synthesized placeholders. The map, town, battle and defeat music, the riding hoofbeats, the sword and arrow sounds and the encounter, victory and legendary stingers are free recorded sounds (CC0/CC-BY, see `AUDIO_CREDITS.md`).

Playback runs on the MLin Audio System package. `GameAudio` in `Runtime/Infrastructure/Audio` is the only adapter: it receives typed successful-gameplay facts from `GameSession` and never determines gameplay. Every sound is a `SoundDefinition` asset (`Content/Audio/Sounds/`) that owns the clips and variants, volume, pitch, priority, instance limit, cooldown and mixer group. `AudioDatabaseScriptableObject` has one field per game event (`Application/SoundCue` enum), per music layer and per music track (`MusicTrack`: battle, defeat), the riding loop, the layer mix, strike timing and the heavy-hit threshold. Each unit references its own voice in the unit database.

One music track leads at a time (`PlayMusic`, crossfaded by `Layer Fade Seconds`): the mission's map music on the adventure map (at `Map Music Idle Level` while the hero stands, full while he rides, moving between them over `Map Music Ride Fade Seconds`), its town music on the town screen (and the hero screen of a hero stationed there), the battle music while fighting, the victory music once after the victory stinger (after `Victory Music Delay Seconds`, fading in over `Victory Music Fade In Seconds`, playing to its end and fading out over its last `Victory Music Fade Out Seconds` while the map or town music returns; a new battle cuts it short) and the defeat theme on the defeat screen. Map and town music are set on the mission (`AshenPass.asset` → Music) so every map can have its own; they resume where they stopped. `UnderworldMusic` is prepared for a future underground map and not assigned yet. The ambient and gallop loops are 100 BPM / 9.6-second WAV files under `Content/Audio/`; they lead only when a mission has no map music and otherwise stay at `Ambient Under Music`. They start sample-aligned on one DSP time (`AudioSystem.PlayLayers`) and their volumes fade with travel state; under the battle and defeat music the ambient drops to `Ambient Under Music`. A battle starts the battle music with `PlayMusic`: the intro clip once, then the looping theme (also on a retry); a won battle fades it out under the victory stinger, a lost one crossfades to the defeat theme until the defeat screen closes. The hoofbeat loop fades in and out with the ride. Friendly and enemy encounters have their own guitar stingers (entering a town, a new week and the mission start keep the short friendly cue). Each strike plays a sword swing or arrow release, then after `Melee/Ranged Impact Seconds` a hit or a miss; heavy hits add the heavy-hit layer, and strikes resolved together (attack and retaliation) are spaced by `Strike Spacing Seconds`. The rewards screen plays the gold cue, plus the legendary choir when a legendary artefact dropped; gold/equip/click use short feedback. Unit voices replace each other instead of overlapping.

Mix: `GameMixer` routes Music, Effects and Voice under Master. Players have independent music, effects and voice volumes (saved by the audio system's built-in PlayerPrefs storage) plus mute; all audio mutes while the window is unfocused. Music ducks 6 dB while a unit speaks (`GameAudioSettings` ducking rule). Instance limits and priorities on the SoundDefinitions prevent unbounded stacking; music layers are never stolen by effects. F3 opens the uGUI development panel that auditions every layer, event sound and unit voice without replaying the mission.

Database and SoundDefinition edits take effect on a new Play Mode initialization or a rebuilt player. New Mission resets campaign state without reloading the catalog.
