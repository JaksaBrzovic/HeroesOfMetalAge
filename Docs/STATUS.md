# Prototype status

Playable prototype 0.7 on Unity 6000.3.26f1 with the Universal Render Pipeline (2D Renderer), targeting Full HD (1920x1080). Runtime is split into five dependency-enforced runtime assemblies (Domain, Application, Presentation, Infrastructure, Bootstrap) plus Editor tooling and an EditMode test assembly.

Delivered: a main menu over the world artwork in parallax depth layers with a camera that follows the mouse and sways in the wind while the mouse rests, with the map music (an animated New Game button with hover and click sounds and a gold pour over its title; Load Game falling into a hell hole with the camera falling in after it, Credits unrolling a sealed scroll into rolling credits, Quit sending a rider off on his chopper before the program closes) and a transition into the mission (fade to black with the music, black, an intro of typed stanzas and a title with the map music fading in, then the black burning away to uncover the map; Load Game loads the saved campaign, or starts the mission without the intro when there is none), the mission's story on a big scroll with an explosion, a shaking screen and a thrown-away scroll, adventure map and route arrows (a new destination can be picked during the ride), day/movement economy with a three-second end-of-day hourglass sequence, joining encounter, blocking encounter, hex combat with simple enemy AI, town recruitment, weekly stock growth, ownership through combat, protected clan home army, owned home/visiting army transfer, hero home/visiting placement, XP, rolled gold and artefact rewards (common/rare/legendary, at most one artefact per victory, a legendary at the latest on every fourth victory), equipment/inventory, level bonuses, strikes that can miss, victory, rewards and defeat screens with retry, save/load, map and town music per mission, battle and defeat music, victory music that loops until the rewards screen closes, riding hoofbeats, sword/arrow swing-hit-miss sounds and creature calls, audio options, dialogues on the map that close with a click anywhere and the F3 development panel.

The adventure map and the battlefield are world objects drawn by an orthographic camera inside viewports of the UI screens. A map is a painted image cut into square cells (pixels per cell is a setting with a Rebuild) with a Tilemap logic layer painted over it; the logic layer is the only terrain the rules read and unpainted cells are impassable. The camera follows the hero while he rides and pans with the arrow keys or by dragging; there is no zoom yet. Stacks glide, strike, flash and fade, damage numbers rise and heavy hits shake the camera. The Ashen Pass map is the painted Map_1 (3200 x 1920, 50 x 30 cells of 64 px) with a first-draft logic layer to be corrected by hand. Movement follows classic strategy games: 1600 points a day, a cost per terrain (road 75 to swamp 175), diagonals 1.41 times dearer, trees, mountains, water, lava and obstacles impassable. The hero rides with a walk cycle, Roadies and Air Guitarists (replacing Ironbound and Ash Archer) walk on the map and battlefield, and battles use the green vale or infernal waste image of their encounter. Saves are version 3; older saves (old map) are rejected.

Audio runs on the MLin Audio System package (v0.2.0): every cue is a `SoundDefinition`, the mixer has Master/Music/Effects/Voice channels, music ducks under unit voices, one music track leads at a time (map, town, battle with intro then loop, defeat), the ambient and gallop layers start sample-aligned and lead only for a mission without map music, strikes play their swing and impact in sequence and all audio mutes while the window is unfocused. Recorded sounds are listed with their licences in `AUDIO_CREDITS.md`. The legendary guarantee counts victories per game session (not saved) so it can be tested within the short first mission.

Data-driven authoring is implemented through AudioDatabaseScriptableObject, UnitDatabaseScriptableObject, ArtefactDatabaseScriptableObject, HeroDataBaseScriptableObject and MissionSettingsScriptableObject. Gameplay uses immutable definitions and encapsulated state; views dispatch commands, audio consumes typed facts and persistence writes stable-ID version 3 snapshots and rejects older saves made on the previous map.

The whole UI, including the F3 development panel, is prefab-authored Canvas/uGUI with TextMeshPro: saved UI prefabs cover the root, the front end (main menu, fade, intro), gameplay screens, the encounter dialog, the victory, rewards and defeat screens, options, header/footer/notifications, the development panel and four shared widgets; the world has its own prefabs (World, Battlefield, WorldMarker, the map prefab). Views reference their controls through typed serialized fields. Prefabs are authored assets; only the bootstrap scene is generated.

Verification: **EditMode tests** (`Tests/EditMode`, run by `Tools/Build.ps1`) cover campaign rules and atomic rejections, battle resolution and replay, routing, persistence and save migration, content validation and authored-data consumption, audio cue resolution and routing, the world (prefabs, logic layer orientation, Rebuild resampling, map cell and battle hex picking, routing on a 256x256 map) and the UI prefabs (initialization against real content, canvas/input setup, fonts, raycasts, nested widgets, the development panel, sprite fallbacks and the absence of script-built UI). The **built-player scenario** (`Tools/SmokeTest.ps1`) plays the whole mission including conquest, home/visiting, save/load, victory, defeat and retry, clicks the map and the board through the world input, checks camera follow, pan limits, viewport alignment, pointer raycasts and resolutions, and captures screenshots that are inspected. Latest results are in `Reports/` (not committed).

The visuals, the synthesized layers/cues and the speech voices are placeholders. Recorded guitar stems and final voice performances remain a creative production milestone. The prototype does not include town construction/investment, negotiations, spells, multiple heroes, partial-stack splitting, multiplayer or campaigns beyond this mission.

Next useful iteration: user playthrough and listening feedback, especially gallop timing, perceived danger, impact intensity, the voice ducking amount, combat pacing and recruitment usability. Then replace placeholder audio and art while preserving the working mission loop.
