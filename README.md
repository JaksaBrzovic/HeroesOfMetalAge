# Heroes of Metal Age — prototype 0.9

Unity **6000.3.26f1**, Windows x64, Mono, Universal Render Pipeline (2D Renderer). An original sound-driven turn-based strategy prototype inspired by classic adventure-map and tactical-combat games. Target resolution: Full HD (1920x1080, 16:9); the UI is designed at 1920x1080 and scales to other sizes.

## Game design

The living [Game Design Document](Docs/GAME_DESIGN.md) describes the game vision, implemented rules and content, audio/visual direction, future systems and open decisions. Update it alongside development. ScriptableObject assets remain the source for actual tuning values.

## Play the prototype

Run `Builds/Windows/HeroesOfMetalAge.exe`, or `Play.cmd` at the project root. No Unity Editor is required to play.

The game opens on the main menu, over the world artwork in eleven depth layers (sky to foreground rocks) and with the map music; the view follows the mouse like a camera, so the nearer layers move more and the scene gets depth, and once the mouse has rested for a random 1 to 4 seconds the camera eases into a gentle sideways sway, as if in the wind, until the mouse moves again (`ParallaxView` on the FrontEnd prefab, layers in `Content/UI/Sprites/MainMenu/Parallax/`). New Game is the arm on the right, just under its title: hovering flexes it so the fist rises over the title while molten gold pours over the title (and drains away when the pointer leaves; every entry's title does the same with the hover sound), a click raises the metal horns and the gold stays, then the screen and the music fade to black, a few seconds of black pass and the intro plays on black: three stanzas typed letter by letter in the middle of the screen, each held and faded out, then the title "The Secret of Metal" fades in slowly while the map music starts with a long fade in, and a few seconds later the black with the title burns away like paper and uncovers the mission. Load Game lies in a hell hole: a click lets its title crumble and fall into the hole, then the camera falls in after it, slowly at first and ever faster, turning a little, until the black inside fills the screen; the black burns away and the saved campaign is loaded (without a save the mission starts at once, without the story). Credits sits on a sealed scroll: a click breaks the seal and unrolls it (rolling it up again on the way back), the credits screen fades in and the credits roll up; any click goes back to the menu. Quit stands over a hooded rider on his chopper: hovering lifts his foot onto the peg, a click (after he has finished lifting it) sends him off into the distance while the music fades, then the program closes (in the Editor play mode stops). Timing, the intro's text and the credits: `Content/Databases/FrontEnd.asset`; the burn's look: `Content/Rendering/IntroBurn.mat`, the gold's: `MenuGild.mat`; look: `Prefabs/UI/FrontEnd.prefab` (art in `Content/UI/Sprites/MainMenu/`); hover and New Game sounds: `Main menu` on the AudioDatabase.

A new mission starts with its story on a big scroll over the map: it unrolls with the first page, half a second later a huge explosion goes off over it with a white flash, a shaking screen and a boom; a click (or Space/Enter) rolls it up and unrolls it with the next page, a click on the last page rolls it up and hurls it spinning off the screen, and the mission starts. Pages: `Briefing Pages` on `AshenPass.asset`; look and timing: `Prefabs/UI/MissionBriefing.prefab`, the explosion's colours: `Content/Rendering/NukeExplosion.mat`.

Click a map cell to preview the route, then click it again or press Space to travel. The part of a new route that today's movement cannot pay for is red, and cells the hero has entered leave the route. Clicking another cell while he rides picks a new destination: he stops in the cell he is riding into with the new route planned, and clicking the new destination again (or Space) before he stops lets him ride on without stopping. The hero rides on roads, dirt, grass and other open ground; trees, mountains, water, lava and obstacles stop him. Each cell costs movement points (road 75, grass and dirt 100, ash and rough ground 125, sand 150, swamp 175; diagonal steps 1.41 times as much) from 1600 per day. Arrow keys or dragging the map (left, right or middle button) look around, the mouse wheel zooms (a cell 32 to 64 pixels, 48 at the start); the camera follows the hero while he rides. Click the hero to open him. While a dialogue (portrait box or speech balloon) is open on the map, a click anywhere else closes it. E ends the day: the painted hourglass (32 frames in `Content/UI/Sprites/EndDay/Frames/`, their lengths on the `EndDayOverlay` prefab) runs out and turns over in the middle of the screen for three seconds with its sound, then the new day begins. H opens the hero. C opens the spell book. Escape opens options or returns from town/hero. M mutes. D (or F3) opens the debug panel in two tabs. GENERAL: SPEED MODE (the hero rides almost instantly; movement is still spent), CLICK TO SKIP INTRO (remembered between runs: a mouse click skips New Game's black, verses and title to the burn, a second click ends the burn), campaign shortcuts (gold, next day, teleport to town, all artefacts, terrain overlay) and dialogue previews; SOUNDS: audition every music track, layer, cue and unit voice. It exists in the Editor and development builds only.

Mission: the expanded Ashen Pass (100 x 61 cells). The green west is a network of forest roads and ten clearings: the Willing (six Air Guitarists) near the start, a road crew of Roadies in the south, gold to pick up, an old chest (1000 gold or 500 XP), the Bloody Colosseum (+2 attack for good), the Iron Bastion (+2 defense), the Shrine of the Horns (+2 morale until the next victory), the old watermill (750 gold a week), a Roadie camp (Roadies for sale, its own weekly stock), forest bandits guarding the south and the Iron Reavers sitting on the Gloves of Metal. The old skald in the south-west tells of the chained maiden and hands over the red key. The pass wardens hold the way through the mountains to Ashen Hold (the castle; its gate is the town). East of it lies the ash land: the ash revenants, a ring in the ash, the bone oracle in the north (the sleeping dragon), the red tent in the south-east, which opens only for the red key, the minotaur pack and behind them the chained maiden (freed, she gives the Chain of the Iron Maiden: "The halls of femdom had been restored."). At the end of the road the Black Banner guards the Gate of Fire, the exit portal.

Goals (the mission panel lists them; a check mark when reached): defeat the Black Banner (required, marked *), free the chained maiden, find the Gloves of Metal, learn of the sleeping dragon, reach level 5. The portal asks to finish the mission and shows how many goals were reached (e.g. 2 / 5) without naming the others; it stays dark until the required goal is reached. Finishing returns to the main menu for now.

Map objects: walking onto a pickup, a chest, the tent or the captive stops the ride there; the hero rides over a lasting place (shrine, mill, camp, skald, oracle, portal) and visits it when it is where he rides to, or when its cell is clicked while he stands on it. Morale (from shrines and artefacts, -3 to +3): with good morale a stack that moved or struck may act once more that round, with bad morale a stack may freeze and lose its turn (10% per point, `Morale Chance` on the mission). The battle chronicle tells when it happens.

Hero growth and magic follow Heroes of Might and Magic III; every number is in `HeroRules.asset`, `HeroClassDatabase.asset` and `SpellDatabase.asset`. Kael is a Warrior (a class without a faction). Each level (the classic experience table x 0.3: level 2 at 300 XP, level 5 at 1380) raises one primary stat by one, picked by the class's chances (attack, defense, spell power, knowledge), and offers two secondary skills: the left one improves a skill he knows, the right one is new while one of his eight places is free; Wisdom comes at the latest every six levels. The level-up dialog opens after the rewards (or the chest) and a click on a card learns the skill. The Warrior can learn Archery, Armorer, Offense, Diplomacy, Estates, Intelligence, Leadership, Logistics, Luck, Mysticism, Pathfinding, Resistance, Sorcery, Tactics and Wisdom (Navigation waits for boats); Kael starts with Basic Leadership and Basic Offense. The hero screen shows the four primary stats, mana, morale and luck, and a SKILLS tab beside the inventory. Mana is knowledge x 10 (raised by Intelligence), regrows by 1 plus Mysticism a day and fills up in the own town. The Hall of Forbidden Hymns on the road near the start sells a spell book (500 gold) and spells; spells above level 2 need Wisdom. Fortune's Anvil gives +1 luck until the next victory. Luck may double (good) or halve (bad) a strike's damage, 1/24 per point.

Battle: blue hexes show reachable cells. Click an empty blue hex to move, or an enemy to attack. Melee units can move to an adjacent reachable hex and attack in one action. Air Guitarists strike at range but deal half damage in melee. Each stack gets one retaliation per round; defending reduces incoming damage by 30%. Turns follow creature speed. SPELL BOOK (or C) opens the spell book on your turn: the hero casts one spell per round, without using up the stack's turn; a spell on a stack or an area then waits for a click on its hex (the valid hexes light up, Escape cancels). With Tactics the battle starts with placing your stacks in the first columns (START BATTLE, NEXT STACK). The clan's town (Lord Varg) and the Black Banner (Morthal) have commanders who cast spells; Resistance may let their spells fail, and SURRENDER pays a commander off (part of the surviving army's price, cheaper with Diplomacy) and leaves the battle with the army. With Diplomacy a much weaker army without a commander may offer to join for gold. AUTO (beside WAR COUNCIL) lets the computer fight your side, spells included, until the battle ends or STOP AUTO is pressed. With Quick combat ticked in the options, battles are not shown: they are fought out at once and the victory or defeat screen opens over the map. Both result screens list the casualties: what each side lost, unit by unit.

Holding the right mouse button shows an info card, as in Heroes 3: over a stack on the battlefield (attack and defense with the hero's and the spells' bonuses, damage, health and the health left of its top creature, speed, morale, luck, abilities, the spells on it and a line of lore), an army slot anywhere, an army on the map, a spell in the spell book or the spell shop (level, mana cost, spell power, duration, what it does at the hero's spell power, whether it can be cast or learned), a skill (all three levels, the hero's marked) and an artefact. Release the button to close it. A unit's lore is `Lore` on its entry in `UnitDatabase.asset`.

Town: choose the recruit quantity and purchase any of three creature types. MAX purchases the largest affordable quantity for each type. Neutral garrison belongs to Lord Varg of the Ashen Clan and cannot be transferred. You can optionally fight the clan to own the town, unlock transfers and reduce recruit prices to 55%. In an owned town, click a filled army slot to transfer its full stack to the other row. Click Kael's hero slot to station him at home or return him to visiting. Stationing merges his army with the home army; return him to visiting before recruitment or travel. Stocks accumulate on days 8, 15, 22, etc.

Campaign saves are stored in `%USERPROFILE%/AppData/LocalLow/Iron Oath Studio/HeroesOfMetalAge/campaign.json`. Safe-state movement, recruitment, equipment and victories autosave. Options also provide manual save/load. A defeat permits retrying with the pre-battle army or returning to prepare. Starting a new mission resets the in-memory campaign; the existing save is replaced by the next autosave.

## CLI workflow

```powershell
./Tools/Build.ps1
./Tools/SmokeTest.ps1
```

`Build.ps1` runs the EditMode tests (`Assets/HeroesOfMetalAge/Tests/EditMode`, results in `Reports/editmode-tests.xml`), regenerates the bootstrap scene and writes the Windows development player. `SmokeTest.ps1` runs the built player with isolated campaign data, plays the whole mission including the victory, rewards and defeat screens and a retry, and captures the world, battle, victory, rewards, defeat, hero, town, options, development-panel and terrain-overlay screens into `Reports/`. The tests also run from Unity's Test Runner window.

## Audio

Audio runs on the [MLin Audio System](https://github.com/JaksaBrzovic/MLinAudioSystem) package (`com.manalink.audiosystem`, pinned to a release tag in `Packages/manifest.json`).

- `Content/Databases/AudioDatabase.asset` has one named field per game event (Victory, Sword Hit, Arrow Miss, Legendary Item…), per music layer, for the battle and defeat music and for the riding loop, each pointing to a `SoundDefinition` in `Content/Audio/Sounds/`. Unit voices are assigned on each unit in `UnitDatabase.asset`. Replace clips, add variations or change volume, pitch, priority, instance limit or cooldown on the SoundDefinition; no code changes.
- `Content/Audio/GameAudioSettings.asset` and `GameMixer.mixer` hold the Master/Music/Effects/Voice channels, the voice-over-music ducking rule and focus muting.
- The mission's map music plays on the adventure map and its town music in the town (`Music` on `AshenPass.asset`); the ambient and gallop layers lead only for a mission without map music (`Layer mix` on the AudioDatabase). The battle music (intro, then loop) plays while fighting and the defeat theme on the defeat screen; the hoofbeat loop plays while the hero rides.
- Strikes play a sword swing or an arrow release, then a hit or a miss (`Strikes` timing on the AudioDatabase); heavy hits add the heavy-hit layer.
- Recorded sounds and their licences are listed in `Docs/AUDIO_CREDITS.md`; CC-BY sounds must be credited.

## Edit content without code

Open `Assets/HeroesOfMetalAge/Content/Databases/` in Unity and select:

- `UnitDatabase.asset`: health, damage, attack/defense, speed, price, weekly growth, portrait, walk cycle and voice sound for each unit.
- `ArtefactDatabase.asset`: name, description, icon (`Content/UI/Sprites/Artefacts/`), kind (weapon, shield, head, gloves, torso, ring, charm = amulet, necklace, sash, boots), rarity (common, rare, epic, legendary) and attack/defense/speed/morale/luck/spell power/knowledge bonuses. The hero wears eleven: helm, both arms, gloves, torso, two rings, amulet, necklace, sash and boots. Artefacts placed in the mission (guarded treasures, pickups, the captive's gift) are never rolled as random spoils.
- `HeroDatabase.asset`: hero name, figure on the map (`Portrait`), face in the hero panel (`Panel Portrait`), the large picture on the hero screen (`Screen Portrait`), map ride cycle, description, class (an ID in the hero class database), the four primary stats at level 1, movement points per day, starting army, starting secondary skills, spell book and spells. Units likewise have a figure (`Portrait`) and a `Card Portrait` for slots, recruitment and the battle panel.
- `HeroRules.asset`: the experience table and its growth, the maximum level, how many secondary skills a hero can know, every skill's name, description, icon and value at Basic, Advanced and Expert (the tooltip says what the value means for each), the luck chance per point, mana per knowledge, the daily mana, the spell level without Wisdom, whether the own town restores mana, Pathfinding's baseline cost and the Diplomacy and surrender prices.
- `HeroClassDatabase.asset`: the hero classes (now the Warrior): the primary stat chances below and from the high level, the level they switch at, how often Wisdom is forced and each secondary skill's weight (0 or missing: never offered).
- `SpellDatabase.asset`: the spells in spell book order: name, description, icon, level, mana cost, price, target (one enemy, one ally, every enemy, every ally, an area, everything), effect (damage, heal, attack, defense, speed, shield, bless, curse), base amount and amount per spell power, base duration and duration per spell power.
- `TerrainDatabase.asset`: which terrain is passable, the movement cost of each and the diagonal step cost.
- `AudioDatabase.asset`: the sound for each game event, music layer and music track, the riding loop, the layer mix, strike timing and the heavy-hit threshold (see Audio above).
- `DialogueDatabase.asset`: the dialogue typing speed, the speakers (ID, name, portrait) and the conversations (ID and lines: ID, speaker ID, text, optional voice-over SoundDefinition on the Voice channel). See Dialogue below.
- `AshenPass.asset`: the map prefab, start and encounter cells, encounter armies (any number of each kind; one town), experience, typical gold and a guarded artefact, a map figure per encounter, an optional commander per hostile encounter (name, spell power, mana, spells in the order cast), the **Map Objects** (kind, cell, figure and its height, gold, experience, artefact, attack/defense/morale/luck, the dwelling's unit and weekly growth, the key a quest giver hands over or a gate needs, a spell shop's spells (its gold is the spell book's price), an arrival conversation), the **Objectives** (text, kind: defeat an encounter, visit an object, own an artefact, reach a level; required or not), starting gold, town/faction, prices, the default and per-encounter battlefield images, combat tuning (including the miss and morale chances), battle reward rolls (including epic) and pacing (travel step, enemy turn, the pause after every battle action and the pause before the victory/defeat screen).
- `Content/UI/Typography.asset`: the game's fonts, see Typography below.

Stable IDs are used by saves and cross-references. Reorder entries freely, but do not rename/remove IDs without migrating their references/saves. Stop and restart Play Mode or rebuild the player to pick up edited definitions. In-game New Mission uses the existing catalog snapshot. Portraits/icons are optional and use the placeholder art from `Content/UI/UiTheme.asset` when unassigned; the theme also holds the hex colours.

Runtime is split into Domain, Application, Presentation, Infrastructure and Bootstrap assemblies under `Assets/HeroesOfMetalAge/Runtime/`. Domain and Application have no Unity references. Views issue validated commands; audio and persistence are separate adapters. See `Docs/HOMA_ARCHITECTURE.md` for ownership, contracts and decisions.

Saves (version 5: secondary skills, level-ups, primary stats, luck, the spell book and mana) use stable IDs; successful replacements retain a `.bak` backup. Version 4 saves load with the hero's starting skills, full mana and every level reached still to be granted (one level-up dialog after the other); older saves are rejected and the game then starts a new mission. Tests and the smoke scenario never use the normal player campaign file.

## Dialogue

Dialogue can open at any time anywhere on the screen, in one of two styles chosen per conversation (`Style`):

- **Portrait box**: a dialogue box with the speaker's portrait on the left and the name and text on the right.
- **Speech balloon**: comic-strip style, small bubbles rise from the speaker to a cloud with the text; no portrait. (In comics a trail of small bubbles is the thought-balloon convention; a speech balloon usually has a pointed tail.)

Text types fast, letter by letter; a click on the dialogue shows the whole line, the next click moves on, and it closes after the last line. A line may have a recorded voice-over.

In the mission: reaching the Willing plays their portrait line before the join dialog appears, reaching the pass wardens plays theirs before the battle dialog, the Black Banner speaks in a balloon from its figure on the map before the battle dialog, and entering Ashen Hold while the clan holds it makes Lord Varg speak in a balloon from his garrison slot. Each encounter in `AshenPass.asset` names its conversation in `Arrival Conversation Id` (empty for none). Escape during an encounter line leaves the encounter as usual. The automated smoke scenario runs without these site conversations.

- Content: `Content/Databases/DialogueDatabase.asset`. Add a speaker, then a conversation with its lines; a single remark is a conversation of one line. IDs must be unique; validation names any problem at startup.

## Adventure map and battlefield

The map and the battle board are drawn in the world by an orthographic camera; the UI stays on top as uGUI and leaves a viewport for them (`MapViewport` in Adventure, `BattleViewport` in Combat).

**Replace the map image and adjust the cells**

1. Import the map image (PNG) anywhere under `Assets/` and open `Content/Maps/AshenPass/AshenPassMap.prefab`.
2. On the root's **Adventure Map Authoring**, set `Map Image` and `Pixels Per Cell` (cells are square). The Ashen Pass image is the expanded map, painted at 1605 x 980 and upscaled 4x with Real-ESRGAN (x4plus) for sharp edges, 6400 x 3904 px at 64 px per cell = 100 x 61 cells; the Inspector shows this for any image.
3. Press **Rebuild**. It imports the image with one cell per world unit, sets the grid and keeps what is already painted: when the cell size changes, every new cell takes the terrain painted under its centre.
4. Paint the logic layer: select `Grid/Terrain Logic`, open **Window > 2D > Tile Palette**, choose the palette **Terrain Logic** and paint the terrain over the image (Road, Grass, Dirt, Rough, Sand, Swamp, Forest, Mountain, Water, Lava, Obstacle). Costs and passability come from `TerrainDatabase.asset`; unpainted cells are impassable. The current layer was derived from the image's colours (`Tools/MapLogic/`): roads and clearings walkable, forest, rock, water and lava blocking, the ash land rough ground, the castle solid but for its gate; check it with the overlay and correct it with the palette.
5. Hover the map in the Scene view to read a cell's coordinates (from the top-left corner) and set the hero start and the encounter positions in `AshenPass.asset`. After changing the cell size, check these positions.

The logic layer is the only terrain the rules use and it is hidden in the game; the F3 panel's **TERRAIN OVERLAY** shows it over the image. `Display Pixels Per Cell` on the map's **Adventure Map View** sets how large a cell appears on screen at the start (48; 0 = the image's own pixel size) and `Zoom Pixels Per Cell` the range the mouse wheel zooms between (32 to 64). The map was painted at a quarter of its pixel size, so it looks sharpest zoomed out. The town of this map is painted on the image, so its marker is hidden (`Show Town Marker`); its cell in front of the gate is still clickable.

**Battlefield**: `Prefabs/World/Battlefield.prefab` holds the fixed background image and the hex board laid over it (`Board Center`, `Hex Width`; select it to preview the hexes in the Scene view). Images are chosen per encounter (`Battle Background` on each encounter in `AshenPass.asset`), else the mission's: the green vale for the pass, the infernal waste for Ashen Hold and the Black Banner.

Figures (`Prefabs/World/WorldMarker.prefab`) stand on their cell with a shadow in their side's colour and play their walk cycle while they move, facing where they go; in battle the stack whose turn it is has a pulsing ring and a highlighted hex. Figures lunge when they strike, flash when hit and fade when a stack dies; damage numbers rise from the target and heavy hits shake the camera. All of this only shows what the rules already resolved.

## Edit UI prefabs

The entire UI uses Unity Canvas/uGUI and TextMeshPro, including the F3 development panel. Open `Assets/HeroesOfMetalAge/Prefabs/UI/GameUiRoot.prefab` in Prefab Mode; edit its nested screen prefabs under `Screens/` and shared controls under `Widgets/`. Every view references its controls through named, typed fields in the Inspector (for example `titleText`, `rideButton`); a missing reference is reported by name when the game starts and by the UI tests.

The screens follow the UI mockups: each screen's painted artwork (cut from its mockup with every text erased and the map or battlefield viewport cut out, `Content/UI/Sprites/Gameplay/<Screen>/`) is its `Backdrop` or `Frame`, and the live texts, images, buttons and slots sit over it. Every full screen has its own `Header` (day, gold, hero, options) and, where its artwork has room, `Footer`. Buttons painted on the artwork are `HotspotButton`s: transparent until hovered, when a golden glow shows; the generic `Button` widget wears the mockups' button frame (9-sliced). `ItemSlot` shows a worn or carried artefact, `ObjectiveRow` a goal with its check mark, `ArmySlot` a stack's card and count. To move something, move it over the artwork in Prefab Mode; to change the artwork, replace the sprite.

The adventure map prefab groups `Background`, `Grid/Terrain Logic`, `Route`, `Towns`, `Friendly Units`, `Enemies`, `Map Objects` and `Heroes`; the battlefield groups `Background`, `Hexes`, `Units/Player Units`, `Units/Enemy Units` and `Effects`. Map object figures are in `Content/World/MapObjects/`: placeholder bronze tokens with an icon (see `Docs/ART_REQUESTS.md` for the art to replace them), the gold, the minotaur and the maiden cut from the mockups.

Runtime creates instances of saved prefabs for markers, route steps, hexes, damage numbers and development-panel buttons; it does not construct controls or draw screens through OnGUI. The prefabs are authored assets: tooling never regenerates them, so Prefab Mode edits are permanent. Only `Assets/Scenes/AshenPass.unity` is generated (Tools > Heroes of Metal Age > Generate scene).

The root retains the legacy EventSystem input module. CanvasScaler Expand fits the 1920x1080 design frame on other aspect ratios with letterboxing. Popup dialogs, options and the development panel block gameplay clicks, including clicks on the map and board, and restore/select keyboard focus.

## Current scope and limitations

Implemented: square adventure grid, diagonal pathfinding without corner cutting, movement budget, days/weeks, friendly joining, blocking hostile encounters, map objects (pickups, chest, shrines, mill, dwelling, quest giver and key gate, captive, oracle, exit portal), mission goals, morale, hex tactical combat, retaliation and ranged troops, misses, simple enemy AI, victory/rewards/defeat screens, XP/levels, rolled gold and artefact rewards with a legendary guarantee and guarded treasures, eleven equipment places and inventory, neutral/owned town recruitment, two army rows, weekly growth, town conquest, save/load and sound-reactive playback with ducking and focus muting.

Art is original procedural placeholder art: the map and battlefield images are generated stand-ins until painted images are imported. The ambient and gallop layers and some short cues are original synthesized placeholders; the battle and defeat music, hoofbeats, strikes and encounter/victory/legendary stingers are free recorded sounds (see `Docs/AUDIO_CREDITS.md`). Creature calls are offline Microsoft David speech placeholders. The final sound experience needs authored guitar stems, performance, mixing and user listening tests. Town construction/investment, multiple playable heroes, factions, schools of magic, adventure spells, boats, campaign selection, multiplayer and drag/drop partial stack splitting are outside this first slice.

All interface text is currently English. The game is not an asset or interface copy of Heroes of Might and Magic III.

Sprite fallback uses Unity null semantics, so unassigned/destroyed references in Editor Play Mode correctly select placeholder art. After code refresh, stop the current Play session and start it again to recreate its presentation context.
